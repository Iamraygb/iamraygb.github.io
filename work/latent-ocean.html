The problem

Variational autoencoders (VAEs) sit underneath a lot of generative AI, but they are usually introduced through probability notation and loss functions. For newcomers that often produces intimidation before it produces intuition.

Our research question was whether a VAE could be reframed as something people already understand: a packing problem, where things compete for limited space.

My role

I designed and built Latent Ocean end to end: the metaphor, the interaction model, the visuals, the animation pipeline and the front end. Dr. Murphy guided the machine-learning content. Together we co-authored the research poster.

Try it

Latent Ocean runs in your browser. Drag the clouds, or use Tab and the arrow keys, and watch the scores change.

Process

  1. Find a metaphor that carries the math

    Latent space becomes an ocean. Each fish image is represented by a translucent cloud, its latent distribution. At the center sits a reef: the safe, shared region every cloud is pulled toward. Each cloud also needs room of its own so it can be told apart from the others. That tension is the trade-off a VAE balances.

    In the oceanIn a VAE
    The oceanLatent space
    A cloudOne image’s latent distribution
    The Safety ReefThe shared center clouds are pulled toward
    Overlap between cloudsCompetition for space

  2. Let people move the idea with their hands

    Instead of reading about the trade-off, learners drag the clouds and watch two measures update live: Safety Reef overlap and Exclusivity. A third score, Reef Harmony, combines them, and a Reef Penalty slider shifts the balance between safety and exclusivity. The page is explicit that Reef Harmony is a designed teaching score, not the model’s actual loss. Being honest about where a metaphor stops is part of teaching well.

  3. Build it so everyone can use it

    Every cloud can be selected with Tab and moved with the arrow keys (Shift for larger steps), with clear focus behavior. Direct manipulation shouldn’t require a mouse.

  4. Prototype fast, with AI as a collaborator

    I used agentic, AI-assisted development with Claude Code to generate and iterate the HTML/CSS front end, then version-controlled and deployed it with Git and GitHub Pages. For the animated latent-space sequences I wrote SVG animations, rendered frames in Python with cairosvg and exported video with ffmpeg.

  5. Let the idea branch

    The project now has two branches: my original, more detailed version and a simpler spin-off Dr. Murphy built from it. Our poster told the story of how one led to the other.

Outcome

Latent Ocean is live on the web, and our co-authored poster was presented at the NCHC 2026 Student Research Showcase.

Next, I want to measure whether people actually learn from it: whether a packing-problem framing builds better intuition than a standard introduction.
